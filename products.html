01 / GST notice review

What it does

For CAs and their teams who handle notices for many clients. Each client has its own workspace for notices, records, drafts and review history.

  1. Upload the notice

    A PDF, a scan, a Word file or a spreadsheet. The original file is kept as the reference.

  2. Check the summary

    Akshi extracts the notice number, dates, reply deadline, tax periods, the sections cited and each amount by tax head. Anything missing, unreadable or uncertain is marked for you to check.

  3. Work through the issues

    The notice is split into separate issues that keep the department's numbering and wording, so the reply can answer point by point.

  4. Reconcile the input tax credit (ITC)

    Upload invoices, the purchase register and the GST returns (GSTR-2A, 2B and 3B) that show what suppliers reported and what the client claimed. Akshi shows what the records support, what does not match and what difference remains, with separate totals for IGST, CGST, SGST and cess.

  5. Weigh a defence

    For a difference the firm disputes, Akshi sets out possible responses. Each one lists the supporting documents, the law relied on, what is still needed, and the weaknesses and adverse authorities.

  6. Review two outputs

    A response draft for the department and an internal review note for the firm. They are kept as separate documents, and the internal note is not part of the submission draft.

  7. Approve

    A named CA at the firm approves the draft version. Until then it stays a working draft.

02 / Limits

What it does not do

Notice review prepares the reply. These steps stay with the firm.

  • Log in to the GST portal or fetch notices. The firm uploads each notice.
  • File a reply with the department.
  • Make a payment or a reversal.
  • Treat acceptance of an ITC amount as acceptance of interest or penalty.
  • Conclude that a client is entitled to ITC without evidence and professional review.

03 / Monthly ITC reconciliation

Catch the mismatch before it becomes a notice

Many ITC notices start with a mismatch between GSTR-2B and the return that nobody caught at filing time.

  1. Every month, for every client

    Before the return is filed, Akshi matches the purchase register against GSTR-2B, the government's statement of what suppliers reported.

  2. A list of exceptions

    Invoices missing from 2B, amounts that differ, and suppliers who have not filed are listed with the money at stake, by tax head.

  3. The firm decides what to claim

    Your team reviews the exceptions, chases suppliers and approves the figures that go into the return.

04 / Notice to appeal

Every open case and its next deadline, across all clients

A notice can run for years: reply, hearing, order, first appeal, then the GST Appellate Tribunal.

  • One list of every case. Each notice, reply, hearing and order, with the date the next step is due.
  • The history stays with the case. When an order arrives, the reply and evidence already on record are there for the appeal.
  • Appeal grounds drafted. Akshi compares the order with the reply and drafts the grounds for the partner to review.
  • The week ahead. The partner sees what is due this week for every client, and who is handling it.

06 / In-house teams

The same product for in-house tax teams

A company with several GST registrations receives the same notices an audit firm handles for its clients. In Akshi each registration gets its own workspace, where a firm would have one for each client.

The tax team uploads the notice and prepares the draft. The head of tax, or the firm that advises the company, is the named approver.
