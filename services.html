01 / Engagements

Three ways to work with us

Most firms start with a review. Each stage stands on its own, and you can stop after any of them.

  1. Review

    We map one workflow with the people who do it: the steps, the hours, the documents, and where errors get caught today. You get a written answer on what agents can do now, what they cannot, and what a build would take.

  2. Build

    We build the workflow and test it against your past cases. You see the results on your own work before your team relies on it.

  3. Run

    We operate the workflow alongside your team. Agents do the work, your people approve it, and we keep testing as rules and inputs change.

02 / Workflows

Two we take on outside tax

Both follow the pattern of tax notice review: split the document into its points, match the evidence, draft, and hand it to the person who signs.

Trademark objection replies

Two frequent objections in an examination report are that the mark is not distinctive, or that it conflicts with an earlier mark. Each needs a different answer, and the reply is due within one month of receiving the report.

Akshi separates the objections, sorts each by its ground, sets the evidence of use or the comparison of marks against it, and drafts the reply. The trademark agent or attorney approves it and files it.

Delayed-payment claims

A micro or small enterprise that qualifies as a supplier under the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development (MSMED) Act, 2006 is owed money, and the buyer is past the due date. The Act gives an eligible supplier interest on the delay and a forum to claim it.

Akshi matches invoices, purchase orders and proof of delivery, works out the overdue amount and the interest, and drafts the claim. The business's CA or advocate reviews and approves it, and the business files it.

03 / Fit

When a workflow is ready for agents

We ask four questions before we take one on. A no to any of them usually means it should wait.

  • Are there past examples? Without finished work to test against, nobody can say whether the output is right.
  • Can a person check the output faster than they could produce it? If review takes as long as the work, there is no saving.
  • Is there a point where a person approves? Something has to stop a wrong answer before it leaves the firm.
  • Does the work come up often enough? A workflow that runs twice a year rarely repays a build.
